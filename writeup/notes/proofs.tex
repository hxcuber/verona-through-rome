\begin{abstract}
This is a companion to \file{writeup/main.tex} (the formal report, which states every definition
and theorem but proves none of them in prose) and to \file{lean/gc/README.md} (which records
\emph{design} decisions, not proofs). This document walks the results that are actually
\emph{proved} in \file{lean/gc/Gc/}: what each theorem says, the shape of its Lean proof, which
invariants it genuinely needs, and — since these are finished mechanized proofs rather than paper
sketches — the lemmas discovered along the way that turned out to be reusable, and the places
where the argument has a real wrinkle a paper proof would gloss over. Where \file{main.tex} already
states a definition or theorem precisely, we cite it by name instead of restating it, and spend the
space on what \file{main.tex} does not have: the actual proof.

Scope: \file{Gc/Model/} and \file{Gc/Reachability/Reachable/}. \file{Gc/Reachability/Referencable/}
is deliberately excluded — it is deprecated and superseded by \file{Reachable/} (see
\file{CLAUDE.md}).
\end{abstract}

\section{\file{Gc/Model}: the operational semantics and its invariants}

\subsection{The model and its rules}

The configuration $\langle S, H\rangle$ (stack of frames, heap of regions) and the nine mutation
operations' operational-semantics rules are given in full in \file{writeup/main.tex}, in its
``Model'' and ``Operational Semantics'' sections.\footnote{This document compiles standalone, so
cross-file \texttt{\textbackslash ref}s to \file{main.tex}'s labels are not used; section names are
given in prose instead.}
We reuse that notation throughout: $\mathsf{RId}(rid)$/$\mathsf{OId}(oid)$ for references,
$a.loc(\langle S,H\rangle)$ for where a reference resolves ($\mathsf{Stk}(fid)$ or $\mathsf{Rgn}(rid)$
or undefined), $a.objAt(\langle S,H\rangle)$ for the object an $\mathsf{OId}$ reference currently
names.

\subsection{The ten invariants of \textit{ValidConfig}}
\label{sec:invariants}

\file{main.tex}'s ``Invariants'' section defines all ten (\textit{L1}, \textit{L2}, \textit{H1},
\textit{H2}, \textit{H3}, \textit{S1}, \textit{S2}, \textit{S3}, \textit{HS1}, \textit{HS2}) and
bundles them into \textit{ValidConfig}. We do not restate
the formulas; the table below is only a one-line reminder of what each one \emph{forbids}, since
that is the fact every proof below actually reaches for.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Invariant & What it rules out \\
\midrule
\textit{L1} & the same object id allocated twice (stack or heap, anywhere) \\
\textit{L2} & a frame executing inside a region that is actually \textit{Closed} \\
\textit{H1} & a region whose bridge object isn't actually a member of its own \textit{objMap} \\
\textit{H2} & two live $\mathsf{RId}(rid)$ references to the same region \\
\textit{H3} & a region's contents holding a reference that resolves \emph{outside} that region \\
\textit{S1} & two stack frames both claiming the same region \\
\textit{S2} & a frame referencing a stack object owned by a \emph{later} frame \\
\textit{S3} & a frame referencing a region owned only by a \emph{later} frame \\
\textit{HS1} & a dangling $\mathsf{OId}$ (points at an id never/no-longer allocated) \\
\textit{HS2} & a dangling $\mathsf{RId}$ (points at a region no longer in the heap) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}
\textit{HS1}/\textit{HS2} are not derivable from the rest: \textit{H2}, \textit{H3}, \textit{S2},
\textit{S3} all presuppose a reference that \emph{already} resolves somewhere; none of them
constrains a reference that resolves to nothing. Without \textit{HS1}/\textit{HS2}, a stale
reference could sit inert and then coincidentally collide with a freshly allocated id later, and
nothing above would have caught the staleness in between. (\file{main.tex} makes the same point;
it surfaced while proving \textit{S2}/\textit{S3} preservation for the three \op{make*}
operations, whose \op{freshObjectId}/\op{freshRegionId} only look at ids currently used as
\emph{keys}.)
\end{remark}

Four corollaries proved directly from the invariants (\file{Validity.lean}) are cited constantly
by everything downstream, in both layers:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{oid\_in\_stack\_implies\_not\_in\_heap} / \texttt{oid\_in\_heap\_implies\_not\_in\_stack}
    — from \textit{L1}: an id found by one search can't also be found by the other.
  \item \texttt{oid\_loc\_rgn\_iff\_in\_heap}, \texttt{oid\_loc\_stk\_iff\_in\_stack},
    \texttt{rid\_loc\_rgn\_iff\_in\_heap} — rewrite the opaque computation
    $\mathsf{OId}(oid).loc(\langle S,H\rangle) = \mathsf{Rgn}(rid)$ into the usable
    $\exists\, region.\ H(rid) = region \wedge oid \inN region.objMap$. Every later file that needs
    to reason about \emph{where} an id lives goes through one of these three, never through the raw
    definition of $.loc(\cdot)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{$RuntimeConfig.start$ satisfies \textit{ValidConfig}}

One root region (Open, bridge object $0$) and one root frame executing in it. Each of the ten
invariants reduces, by unfolding, to a fact about a one- or two-element list, closed by
\texttt{decide}/\texttt{simp}/\texttt{List.nodup\_singleton}. No interesting content — this file
exists purely to anchor the induction below.

\subsection{The preservation proof pattern}
\label{sec:preservation-pattern}

Every \file{Gc/Model/Mutation/<Op>.lean} defines the operation as an $Option\ RuntimeConfig$-valued
\texttt{do}-block (each bind is a precondition that can fail — exactly the premises of the
corresponding inference rule in \file{main.tex}), paired with an \op{<op>\_cases} lemma that
unpacks a successful run into an explicit disjunction of fully-instantiated hypotheses, one
disjunct per inference-rule variant. This is mechanical but load-bearing: it is, in effect, the
operation's real specification — a mistake here (a missing disjunct, a wrong side condition)
would not surface as a Lean error in its own short proof (which just replays the \texttt{do}-block's
control flow); it would surface only as an unprovable, or vacuously provable, downstream theorem.
Every proof from here on, in both layers, destructs a hypothesis $op\ cfg = \mathsf{some}\ cfg'$ via
\op{<op>\_cases} exactly once, then argues with concrete named values for the rest.

\file{Preservation/<Op>.lean} then proves ten lemmas $\op{<op>\_L1}, \dots, \op{<op>\_HS2}$ and
combines them:
\[
  \op{<op>\_valid} : \mathit{ValidConfig}(cfg) \to op\ cfg = \mathsf{some}\ cfg' \to \mathit{ValidConfig}(cfg')
\]
\file{Preservation.lean} aggregates all nine into $\mathit{AllPreserve}(\mathit{ValidConfig})$ by
case-splitting on the reified $Stmt$ type (\file{Mutation/Stmt.lean}) — the statement ``every one of
the nine operations preserves \textit{ValidConfig}'' proved once, generically, rather than left as
nine theorems a caller has to know to look up individually.

\subsection{Per-operation notes}

For each operation, the genuinely interesting invariant(s); the rest are short ``nothing relevant
changed'' arguments.

\begin{description}
  \item[\op{varAsgn x yf}] Two branches on whether \texttt{x} is the frame's own bridge variable.
    If so, this is really a \emph{bridge-object reassignment} — subtle because it changes
    \textit{H1}'s witness for that region without touching $objMap$ at all; the proof shows the
    \emph{new} bridge id is still a key of the (unchanged) $objMap$, which falls straight out of the
    rule's own side condition ($yfRef.loc(\cdot) = \mathsf{Rgn}(rid)$ with $rid = frame.regionId$ —
    you can only rebind the bridge to something already inside the same region). If not the bridge
    var, it's a plain local write gated by $resolveV(\texttt{x}, \cdot) = \epsilon$ (no existing
    binding), which exists purely to keep $resolveV$ (outward frame search) unambiguous.
  \item[\op{fieldAsgn xf y}] The \op{field-asgn-stack}/\op{field-asgn-region} split is the clearest
    illustration of why \textit{H3} needs an explicit $status = Open$ side condition: a region write
    is permitted only while the writer's frame executes \emph{in} that exact region \emph{and} the
    new value resolves into the \emph{same} region. Drop either equality and \textit{H3}'s
    preservation becomes false — nothing else in the rule would stop the region from acquiring an
    outward-pointing reference.
  \item[\op{swap x yf}] Syntactically the busiest rule (two locations get new values
    simultaneously) but its invariant proofs are \op{fieldAsgn}'s field-write argument and
    \op{varAsgn}'s var-write argument run side by side — no new invariant-level content.
  \item[\op{makeObjStack x} / \op{makeObjRegion x}] Allocate a fresh, empty object at
    $\langle S,H\rangle.freshObjectId$. \textit{L1} preservation is exactly
    \texttt{RuntimeConfig.freshObjectId\_not\_mem} (\file{Helpers.lean}): the fresh id is, by
    construction, not already in $cfg.objectIds$. \textit{H2}/\textit{HS1} for the new id are
    vacuous (nothing points at it yet). \op{makeObjRegion} additionally needs $status = Open$ — an
    object can only be born directly inside a region while someone is executing there.
  \item[\op{makeRegion x}] Allocates a brand-new \emph{Closed} region with its own fresh bridge
    object, and stores an $\mathsf{RId}$ to it in the current frame. \textit{H2} for the new region
    id is again vacuous by freshness (\texttt{freshRegionId\_not\_mem}); starting \emph{Closed} is
    what keeps \textit{L2} trivially true — nobody is executing inside the new region yet.
  \item[\op{merge x}] By far the busiest file ($\sim$1200 lines, \file{Preservation/Merge.lean}),
    being the only operation that deletes a heap key and combines two $objMap$s into one. The
    load-bearing fact, proved first and reused by all ten invariant proofs, is that the two regions
    being merged have \emph{disjoint} object-id sets:
    \begin{lemma}[disjointness of the merged regions]
    If $H(rid) = region$, $H(rid') = region'$, $region.status = Open$, $region'.status = Closed$,
    and $\langle S,H\rangle \vdash L1$, then $rid \ne rid'$ (Open/Closed are mutually exclusive) and
    $dom(region.objMap) \cap dom(region'.objMap) = \emptyset$ (else \textit{L1} would be violated).
    \end{lemma}
    Disjointness turns the potentially lossy $AList.union$ (which can silently drop/reorder
    colliding keys in general) into a plain, permutation-preserving append, and every subsequent
    \textit{L1}/\textit{H2}-style counting argument goes through by permutation-invariance of
    \texttt{List.count}/\texttt{Nodup}. A second recurring fact re-derives ``an id lives in at most
    one heap entry'' for the \emph{post}-merge heap, by case-splitting on whether each of two
    witnessing entries is the merged head or came from the untouched remainder (which embeds back
    into the original heap via a \texttt{List.Perm} argument built from two nested
    \texttt{List.exists\_of\_kerase} extractions). \op{merge} is also the one operation whose
    \emph{reachability}-layer proof (\S\ref{sec:frameatlaterframe-merge}) needs a genuinely
    different idea from the other eight, because it is the only operation that deletes a region and
    a live $\mathsf{RId}$ reference along with it.
  \item[\op{enter xf bridgeVar} / \op{exit}] Push/pop a frame, flipping the
    entered/exited region's status. \textit{L2}/\textit{S1} are index bookkeeping; the freshly
    pushed frame starts with empty $objMap$/$varMap$, so \textit{S2}/\textit{S3}/\textit{HS1} need
    say nothing new about it — its only content is the just-flipped bridge object, already covered
    by the region-side invariants.
\end{description}

\subsection{Reusable toolkit: \file{Preservation/Common.lean}}

Five generic lemmas, factored out once the same fact was being reconstructed byte-for-byte in
several files:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{stackWithIndex\_frame\_transport\_down\_of\_shape\_eq} / \texttt{\_up\_of\_shape\_eq}
    — if $cfg,cfg'$ agree up to some projection $\pi : Frame \to \alpha$ at every stack
    \emph{position}, $stackWithIndex$ membership transports between them, preserving $.index$ and
    the projected value. Generic in $\pi$: a proof that only cares about $(regionId, bridgeVar)$ can
    ignore $objMap$/$varMap$ changes entirely.
  \item \texttt{stackWithIndex\_objMap\_get\_eq\_of\_last\_varMap\_update} — the common special case
    ``only the last frame's $varMap$ changed'' $\Rightarrow$ $objMap$ is unaffected at every
    position, indexed or not. Used by every pure var-write operation.
  \item \texttt{merge\_corollary\_regionId\_unique\_index} — \textit{S1} restated at the
    $FrameWithIndex$ level (two frames sharing a $regionId$ are the same frame). Despite the name
    (kept from wherever it was first needed) this is used throughout \S\ref{sec:reachability-lemmas}
    to convert ``same region'' facts into ``same index'' facts, which is what most confinement
    arguments actually need.
  \item \texttt{swap\_corollary\_stackWithIndex\_index\_inj} / \texttt{\_find\_eq} — two frames with
    the same $.index$ are the \emph{same} element (since $.index$ is assigned by position, not
    stored data). Arguably the single most-cited lemma in \file{Gc/Reachability/Reachable/} — see
    \S\ref{sec:reusable}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pitfalls specific to this layer}

\begin{pitfall}[\texttt{AList.insert} reorders on an existing key]
Inserting at an already-present key goes through $kinsert$/$kerase$ and can reorder $.entries$,
unlike inserting at a genuinely fresh key. Any proof needing $.entries$-level equality after an
$insert$ (not just $.lookup$-level) — \op{merge}'s region-combining argument above — has to go
through an explicit \texttt{List.Perm} argument (\texttt{exists\_of\_kerase} +
\texttt{NodupKeys.eq\_of\_mk\_mem}), never assume ``insert = append.''
\end{pitfall}

\begin{pitfall}[\textit{Status} has no \texttt{LawfulBEq}]
$Status$ derives $BEq$/$DecidableEq$ but not $LawfulBEq$, so \texttt{beq\_iff\_eq} doesn't fire on
it. Every ``Open xor Closed'' contradiction in this codebase is closed by case-splitting
$region.status$ directly and \texttt{decide}, never by rewriting a \texttt{==} hypothesis.
\end{pitfall}

\begin{pitfall}[the \op{<op>\_cases} lemma \emph{is} the semantics]
Because the operation is opaque \texttt{do}-notation, when in doubt about an operation's actual
precondition, read its \op{<op>\_cases} lemma's statement, not the \texttt{do}-block — a mistake in
the former is invisible until a downstream theorem quietly stops being provable.
\end{pitfall}

\section{\file{Gc/Reachability/Reachable}: the reachability layer}
\label{sec:reachability}

\subsection{What's already in \file{main.tex}}

\file{main.tex}'s ``Reachability'' section already gives, in full, the definitions of
$\mathit{ReachableStep}$ ($a \to b$), $\mathit{RegionReachable}$, $\mathit{FrameReachable}$,
$\mathit{StackReachable}$, $\mathit{FrameRoot}$; the two ``is exactly the reflexive-transitive
closure of $\to$'' theorems; and (its ``Suspended regions'' subsection) $\mathit{FRALF}$ together
with its single-step preservation theorem and the headline suspended-region stack-reachability
invariant. We do not restate any of these; we only fix the two facts about $\mathit{ReachableStep}$
that everything below leans on, then move straight to how the theorems are actually proved.

\begin{remark}[the one thing $\mathsf{RId}$ can do]
$\mathsf{RId}(rid) \to b$ only when $H(rid).status = Closed$, in which case $b$ is forced to be
$\mathsf{OId}(H(rid).bridgeObjectId)$ — a Closed region's $\mathsf{RId}$ has exactly one outgoing
step, into its own bridge object; an \emph{Open} region's $\mathsf{RId}$ has none
(\texttt{open\_rid\_no\_step}, from \textit{L2}: if a region's $\mathsf{RId}$ could step it would
have to be Closed, contradicting an on-stack frame owning it). This one asymmetry — the ``portal''
into a suspended region — is the entire reason this layer exists as a sibling to the deprecated
$RefStep$ rather than a refinement of it, and it is what makes \S\ref{sec:cr6} non-trivial.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[why the $\xrightarrow{*}$-reformulation matters]
$\mathit{RegionReachable}$/$\mathit{FrameReachable}$ are small inductive relations, but almost every
nontrivial proof below inducts on their \texttt{Relation.ReflTransGen}-reformulation instead (the
``is exactly the transitive closure'' theorems from \file{main.tex}), never on the raw inductive
constructors. \texttt{ReflTransGen} comes with two mathlib induction principles: ordinary
constructor induction (fixes the chain's \emph{first} element, grows the \emph{last} by one step at
a time — the right shape for ``trace forward from a known root'') and
\texttt{head\_induction\_on} (fixes the \emph{last} element, peels off the \emph{first} step — the
right shape for ``chase an escaping chain hop by hop from a known start''). Both get used below,
for different lemmas; picking the wrong one does not produce a type error, it produces a stuck goal
whose motive silently doesn't generalize the variable the argument actually needs to induct on.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The confinement toolkit (\file{Lemmas/Common.lean})}
\label{sec:reachability-lemmas}

This file is the layer's real intellectual core. Every per-operation proof in
\file{FrameReachableAtLaterFrame/} and \file{StackReachableInvariant/} cites several of these
directly.

\begin{lemma}[backward confinement, Open region]
\label{lem:predecessor-open}
\textup{(\texttt{predecessor\_of\_region\_object})} If $\mathsf{OId}(oid).loc(\cdot) = \mathsf{Rgn}(rid)$
and $H(rid).status = Open$, then any $a$ with $a \to \mathsf{OId}(oid)$ is itself
$\mathsf{OId}(oid')$ for some $oid'$ with $oid'.loc(\cdot) \in \{\mathsf{Rgn}(rid)\} \cup
\{\mathsf{Stk}(fid) \mid fid\}$ — never a bare $\mathsf{RId}$, never an object of a
\emph{different} region.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Case on $a$. If $a = \mathsf{RId}(rid')$: either $rid' = rid$, ruled out directly by
\op{open\_rid\_no\_step} above, or $rid' \ne rid$, in which case the only possible step target is
$rid'$'s own bridge object, whose location is $\mathsf{Rgn}(rid')$ by \textit{H3}, contradicting
$\mathsf{Rgn}(rid)$. If $a = \mathsf{OId}(oid')$: \textit{H3} applied to whatever region $oid'$ turns
out to live in forces its location to be $\mathsf{Rgn}(rid)$ too (region case), or it's already on
the stack (stack case) — never a third region.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[backward confinement, Closed region]
\label{lem:predecessor-closed}
\textup{(\texttt{predecessor\_of\_closed\_region\_object})} Same statement with $Closed$ in place
of $Open$, except the ``on stack'' branch becomes \emph{impossible} instead (\textit{L2}'s
contrapositive: an on-stack frame can never own a Closed region — \texttt{closed\_region\_not\_owned}),
and the $\mathsf{RId}$ branch becomes the desired \emph{portal hop} instead of a contradiction: $a =
\mathsf{RId}(rid)$ with $oid = H(rid).bridgeObjectId$ is now an admissible predecessor.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[safety and its transport]
\label{lem:saferef}
Define $\mathit{SafeRef}_{rid}(ref)$ for $ref = \mathsf{OId}(oid)$ as $oid.loc(\cdot) = \mathsf{Rgn}(rid)$
or $oid.loc(\cdot) = \mathsf{Stk}(fid)$ for some $fid$ (never true for a bare $\mathsf{RId}$). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item (\texttt{predecessor\_safe}) safety propagates \emph{backward} across one step, by
    Lemma~\ref{lem:predecessor-open};
  \item (\texttt{safe\_reflTransGen\_transport}) if $\to_{cfg}$ and $\to_{cfg'}$ agree on every
    $\mathsf{OId}$-sourced step, any $cfg$-chain ending at a \textit{SafeRef} target transports
    wholesale to a $cfg'$-chain.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
This pair is exactly the tool used for every operation whose mutation changes no existing object's
\emph{content} — see the ``additive'' case in \S\ref{sec:fralf-technique}.

\begin{lemma}[index confinement]
\label{lem:index-confinement}
\textup{(\texttt{stack\_container\_confined}, \texttt{region\_container\_confined})} If
$\mathsf{OId}(oid_C)$ is stack-resident at index $fid_C$ (resp. resides in an \emph{Open} region
owned by frame index $fid_C$), then anything $\mathit{FrameReachable}(fid, \mathsf{OId}(oid_C))$
has $fid \ge fid_C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Structural induction on the $\mathit{FrameReachable}$-witness's \texttt{ReflTransGen} chain,
generalized over the chain's \emph{end} (ordinary induction, not \texttt{head\_induction\_on} — the
claim is about the chain's \emph{root}, which the inductive step naturally exposes). Each step
either stays inside the confined container (index unchanged, recurse) or escapes onto the stack,
at which point the one-hop bound $S2/S3$-at-the-\texttt{ReachableStep}-level lemmas
(\texttt{stack\_step\_index\_le}, \texttt{stack\_step\_region\_index\_le}) fire directly.
\end{proof}
This is the mechanized form of ``you can't reach backward in time'', and it is what every
per-operation proof in both \S\ref{sec:fralf-technique} and~\S\ref{sec:stackreachable} calls to
turn ``was the mutated container already visible from here'' into a plain numeric comparison.

\begin{lemma}[Closed-region confinement — the engine behind \S\ref{sec:cr6}]
\label{lem:closed-confinement}
\textup{(\texttt{RegionReachable\_of\_FrameReachable\_closed},
\texttt{FrameReachable\_rid\_of\_closed\_container})} If $H(rid).status = Closed$ and
$oid.loc(\cdot) = \mathsf{Rgn}(rid)$, then \emph{any} $\mathit{FrameReachable}(fid,\mathsf{OId}(oid))$
witness is, in fact, $\mathit{RegionReachable}(rid, \mathsf{OId}(oid))$, \emph{and} it forces
$\mathit{FrameReachable}(fid, \mathsf{RId}(rid))$ too — reaching anything inside a Closed region is
never possible except by first reaching the region's own portal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Backward induction over the chain, dispatching at each step on
Lemma~\ref{lem:predecessor-closed}'s two cases: either continue tracing inside the region, or hit
the portal hop and stop.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The \textit{CR6} theorems}
\label{sec:cr6}

Not stated in \file{main.tex}; proved directly from Lemma~\ref{lem:closed-confinement}, no new
technique.

\begin{theorem}[\textit{CR6}]
Let $H(rid).status = Closed$ and $oid.loc(\cdot) = \mathsf{Rgn}(rid)$.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item If $\langle S,H\rangle \vdash SReachable(\mathsf{RId}(rid))$, then
    $SReachable(\mathsf{OId}(oid)) \iff RReachable(rid, \mathsf{OId}(oid))$.
  \item If $\langle S,H\rangle \not\vdash SReachable(\mathsf{RId}(rid))$, then
    $\langle S,H\rangle \not\vdash SReachable(\mathsf{OId}(oid))$ for \emph{every} such $oid$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Part 1's forward direction doesn't even need the $\mathsf{RId}$-reachability hypothesis
(closedness alone forces the single-portal argument); the hypothesis is needed only to glue the
portal hop onto an already-stack-rooted chain in the reverse direction. Part 2 is (1)'s
contrapositive, given its own name because it is the practically useful reading: an unreachable
Closed region's contents are \emph{all} unreachable, full stop.

\subsection{Proving $\mathit{FRALF}$ preservation: three proof shapes}
\label{sec:fralf-technique}

$\mathit{FRALF}$ (defined in \file{main.tex}, see above) is proved to hold vacuously
at $RuntimeConfig.start$ (a single frame makes $F.fid < F'.fid$ unsatisfiable), then
single-step-preserved by all nine operations. The nine proofs split into three genuinely different
argument shapes.

\paragraph{1. Additive / status-only operations — \op{Enter}, \op{Exit}, \op{makeObjStack},
\op{makeObjRegion}, \op{makeRegion}, \op{varAsgn}.} No existing object's \emph{content} changes.
The workhorse is Lemma~\ref{lem:saferef}(2): show the tracked object is $\mathit{SafeRef}$, show the
step relation agrees on every $\mathsf{OId}$-sourced step (trivial when nothing's content changed),
and the whole chain transports for free — no per-hop case analysis. \op{Enter}'s proof is the most
involved member of this group only because pushing a frame means the ``later frame'' in the
$\mathit{FRALF}$ statement might now instead \emph{be} the freshly pushed one, whose only possible
root is the entered region's own bridge object; a short safety argument shows that can't equal the
tracked object in the (different) suspended region.

\paragraph{2. Content-mutating operations — \op{fieldAsgn}, \op{swap}.} These genuinely rewrite one
existing reference slot (call the mutated object's id $oid_C$), so the step relation changes at
exactly that one point and Lemma~\ref{lem:saferef}'s uniform transport no longer applies. The proof
instead:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item shows $oid_C$'s \emph{owner} index is a hard lower bound on anything that can reach it
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:index-confinement}, now applied to the mutated object rather than the tracked
    one) — call this bound $fid_{active}$;
  \item for any chain confined strictly below $fid_{active}$, the step relation is untouched, so it
    transports for free;
  \item for the one remaining case — a chain rooted exactly at the mutating frame, which
    \emph{might} use the new edge — walks the chain forward via \texttt{head\_induction\_on},
    splitting at each hop on whether it is the newly-written one. If it is, the chain escapes onto
    the field's \emph{new} value, which has its own independent root
    (\texttt{resolveV\_frameRoot}/\texttt{resolveFA\_frameReach}), and the argument reduces back to
    case 2.
\end{enumerate}
This is exactly \file{CLAUDE.md}'s ``owner-index bound turns `could this new edge matter' into a
decidable dichotomy, resolved by chasing the new value's own independent root'', traced through
concretely.

\paragraph{3. \op{merge} — its own shape.}
\label{sec:frameatlaterframe-merge}
\op{merge} is the only operation that \emph{deletes} a live $\mathsf{RId}(rid')$ reference along
with the region it names. The key trick:

\begin{lemma}[an about-to-be-deleted region reference is never a step \emph{target}]
\textup{(\texttt{merge\_ridPrime\_not\_step\_target})} If $\mathsf{RId}(rid')$ occurs live in
$\langle S,H\rangle$ (as it must, to be merged), no reference steps \emph{into} it: any putative
predecessor would push \textit{H2}'s count for $rid'$ to $2$, contradiction.
\end{lemma}

Consequently $\mathsf{RId}(rid')$ can only ever be a chain's \emph{root}, never mid-chain, so
``does this chain use the deleted reference'' is answered by inspecting the root alone, never by a
hop-by-hop induction. This gives a chain-avoidance transport
(\texttt{merge\_chain\_transport\_down}: any $\mathsf{RId}(rid')$-avoiding chain transports
unconditionally $cfg' \to cfg$) plus one genuine escape case, handled by

\begin{lemma}
\textup{(\texttt{merge\_ridPrime\_reflTransGen\_iff})} A $\mathsf{RId}(rid')$-rooted chain to any
target $\ne \mathsf{RId}(rid')$ is the same chain, one hop shorter, rooted at
$H(rid').bridgeObjectId$ instead — since $\mathsf{RId}(rid')$'s only possible first hop is that
fixed bridge object.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The suspended-region stack-reachability invariant}
\label{sec:stackreachable}

The headline theorem (stated in \file{main.tex}, see above) is stated as an $\iff$, not a one-way
implication, and takes $\mathit{FRALF}$ as
an \emph{explicit hypothesis} rather than folding it into a \textit{ValidConfig}-style bundled
invariant — this keeps its own proof independent of whether $\mathit{FRALF}$ has separately been
shown inductive, at the cost of every caller needing to supply it.

Each per-operation proof in \file{StackReachableInvariant/} uses \emph{exactly} the same case
analysis as its \S\ref{sec:fralf-technique} sibling — compare, e.g., the two \op{fieldAsgn} files:
same lemmas, same split on whether the frame in question is the active one, same escape argument —
because ``was $oid$ reachable before'' and ``is it reachable after'' are genuinely symmetric
questions once confined transport exists in both directions. The forward direction of the $\iff$ is
close to a direct instance of $\mathit{FRALF}$-style transport; the \emph{backward} direction is
where the escape-chain argument (\S\ref{sec:fralf-technique}, cases 2/3) actually earns its keep,
since going from $cfg'$ back to $cfg$ is where a chain might have used the newly-written edge.

\begin{pitfall}[an $\iff$ needs both halves proved independently]
It is tempting to think the $cfg' \to cfg$ direction of the escape argument is just the $cfg \to
cfg'$ direction ``with the arguments flipped''. It is not: the newly-written edge exists in $cfg'$
but not $cfg$, so the escape case genuinely only arises in one of the two directions and has to be
proved on its own terms there.
\end{pitfall}

\subsection{What's deliberately not done here}
No analogue of a \op{Guarantees.lean}-style top-level GC-safety theorem sits on top of
$\mathit{StackReachableInvariant}$ yet. Everything above is a \emph{single-step} fact; composing it
over an arbitrary-length trace — the literal \file{main.tex}/report.pdf claim, which talks about
invariance for as long as a region stays suspended, not just across one operation — is not
currently being pursued.

\section{Lemmas load-bearing across both layers}
\label{sec:reusable}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \texttt{swap\_corollary\_stackWithIndex\_index\_inj} — ``same index $\Rightarrow$ same
    element'' for $stackWithIndex$. Trivial to state, indispensable once a proof juggles three or
    four differently-named frame variables that are provably-but-not-syntactically the same frame
    (a recurring situation throughout \S\ref{sec:fralf-technique}'s per-operation files).
  \item Lemma~\ref{lem:index-confinement} — turns \emph{every} ``can this later/mutated thing be
    reached from there'' question into a numeric comparison, which is what makes the case splits in
    \S\ref{sec:fralf-technique} decidable rather than requiring genuine reachability search.
  \item The ``\textit{H2} forbids a reference from being a step target'' trick
    (\S\ref{sec:frameatlaterframe-merge}) generalizes beyond \op{merge}: any future operation that
    \emph{deletes} a uniquely-referenced region reference can reuse the same shape (count-based
    non-membership $\Rightarrow$ chain-position restriction $\Rightarrow$ transport reduces to a
    root-only case split).
  \item The $\xrightarrow{*}$-reformulation theorems (\file{main.tex} \S 5) exist purely so nothing
    downstream ever inducts on the custom inductive relations directly.
\end{itemize}

\section{Pitfalls specific to reachability-confinement arguments}

(Complements, does not repeat, \file{CLAUDE.md}'s general ``Proof-engineering gotchas'' list — these
are specific to the confinement style of Part~\ref{sec:reachability}.)

\begin{pitfall}[Open-only tools misapplied to a Closed region]
Lemma~\ref{lem:index-confinement} (region variant) takes $status = Open$ as a hypothesis, because
its proof leans on Lemma~\ref{lem:predecessor-open}, which is stated only for the Open case (an
object inside a \emph{Closed} region has no stack-escape branch to bound in the first place). For a
Closed region the right tool is Lemma~\ref{lem:closed-confinement}/\S\ref{sec:cr6} instead. Reaching
for the Open-only lemma on a Closed region fails to apply outright rather than silently giving a
wrong bound — but it is an easy mismatch when adapting an existing per-operation proof to a new
operation.
\end{pitfall}

\begin{pitfall}[$\mathit{loc}(\cdot)$ is treated as opaque]
The raw definition of $a.loc(\langle S,H\rangle)$ (nested reverse-stack-search / heap-search with a
match forcing exactly one to succeed) is unfolded exactly once, in \file{Validity.lean}, to prove
the three \texttt{\_iff\_in\_*} restatement lemmas of \S\ref{sec:invariants}. Every later file
treats $.loc(\cdot)$ as an opaque function characterized by those iffs, never by unfolding its
definition again.
\end{pitfall}
